Membership

Becoming a Member

Membership in Alpha Phi Alpha Fraternity, Inc.® is a lifelong commitment to leadership, service, scholarship and excellence. It is not a title you receive — it is a standard you uphold every day.

I Am Ready — Begin with the Fraternity Review eligibility UIowa policies
Before you begin

Ask yourself

The Fraternity asks every prospective member to consider these questions. If you can answer “yes,” you may be prepared to pursue membership.

An Alpha Phi Alpha member’s greatest interest is outside himself. Becoming a member requires an investment of your personal time, talents, and money.

  • Are you already a leader in your university or community?
  • Are you committed to a lifetime of service and excellence?
  • Can you meet the expectations of Alpha Phi Alpha?
  • Can you afford the financial commitment that accompanies membership?
Requirements

Eligibility requirements

Alpha Theta is a college chapter seated at the University of Iowa. Under the Fraternity’s requirements, any male student of an accredited college may be presented as a candidate for membership in a college chapter provided that he:

  • Attends an institution where an active chapter is located. Membership is not available for individuals attending an online university that does not have a chapter seat; it is only available for individuals attending the school where the chapter seat is located.
  • Has successfully completed one semester or two quarters of a regular year’s program.
  • Is a full-time student taking courses leading to his first academic degree.
  • Is in good academic standing with a cumulative grade point average of not less than 2.5 on a 4.0 scale or equivalent — except where the college’s minimum grade for graduation is greater, in which case that minimum is used.
  • Is of good character.
  • Is registered to vote, if eligible and qualified.
  • Is sponsored by a member in good standing from the chapter in which he is seeking membership.
  • Is recommended by a member in good standing from the chapter in which he is seeking membership.

Alumni chapter membership

Men who have already earned a four-year baccalaureate degree from an accredited institution may pursue membership through an alumni chapter. See the national membership page for the full alumni requirements. In the Iowa City and Cedar Rapids area, the alumni chapter is Nu Chi Lambda.

How membership is conferred

The official intake process

Alpha Phi Alpha conducts membership intake only through the process set by its Corporate Headquarters.

  1. Official contact

    Communication comes from Corporate Headquarters

    The only official and sanctioned contact regarding intake comes from the Corporate Headquarters of Alpha Phi Alpha Fraternity, Inc. via the director of membership services. Candidates should only participate in membership activities when the date, time and place have been established by the Corporate Headquarters.

  2. Conducted by certified officers

    Certified Intake Officers

    All membership intake activities are conducted by the certified Intake Officer and occur in the presence of a certified Regional Intake Officer.

  3. No added conditions

    The same terms for everyone

    No individual member or chapter of the Fraternity may require additional or different terms or conditions of membership for a candidate or group of candidates.

Pledging policy

No pledging. No hazing.

Pledging is against the purposes and goals of the Fraternity and has been discontinued as a condition or manner of initiation into membership. It is no longer legal within Alpha Phi Alpha for members to establish a “pledge line” or to require a candidate to submit to hazing.

Anyone who participates in hazing a candidate does not represent Alpha Phi Alpha Fraternity, Inc., and may face criminal charges, personal liability and severe disciplinary action by the Fraternity.

A note on terms: on social media you may see brothers refer to their intake class as a “line” or “ship.” That is shorthand for a group initiated together through the official Membership Intake Process. It does not mean a pledge line.

University of Iowa

Campus membership policies

In addition to the Fraternity’s requirements, joining a National Pan-Hellenic Council (NPHC) organization at the University of Iowa follows the policies of Fraternity & Sorority Life (FSL).

Before intake

Attend an NPHC Orientation

All students who want to be considered for membership in an NPHC organization must first attend an NPHC Orientation hosted by FSL. Sessions are offered twice each fall and spring semester, and attendance counts for one calendar year.

At interest meetings

Sign the university forms

Everyone attending a rush or interest meeting signs a Grade Release Form, which lets FSL verify academic eligibility, and a Hazing Policy Notification Form. The chapter submits these to FSL within 48 hours.

Eligibility

Credit hours and timing

Each NPHC organization sets its own semester-hour requirement, ranging from 12 to 30 hours. Alpha Phi Alpha requires at least one completed semester. FSL also delays joining for first-year students during the first three weeks of the fall semester.

During intake

Campus intake standards

Intake takes place only during the academic semester — never over summer or winter break. Activities may not interfere with classes, must be alcohol-free, must have a chapter advisor present, and may not be held in a private residence without FSL approval.

After joining

New Member Academy

New members accept the chapter’s roster invitation and sign their Gold Card within one week. They complete FSL’s New Member Academy — four online modules on ICON — within one month, and their first Violence Prevention Education tier the following semester. Alpha Theta reported 100% New Member Academy completion in Spring 2025.

Costs

What FSL publishes

FSL reports NPHC new-member dues ranging from $1,200 to $2,000 (average $1,680), plus $15 per semester in NPHC council dues and a $25 per semester FSL programming fee billed to your UBill. These are campus-wide NPHC ranges, not Alpha Phi Alpha’s specific fees.

Chapter standards and HEGAP

Every chapter at Iowa is reviewed through the Hawkeye Excellence and Goal Achievement Program (HEGAP). It looks at ten focus areas, including academic achievement, civic engagement, health and safety, and membership growth and development.

  • Standards Achievement: the chapter meets standards in six or more focus areas.
  • Hawkeye Excellence Chapter: the chapter exceeds standards in six or more focus areas.
  • Intake approval: a chapter cannot conduct intake without an FSL-approved Growth and Sustainability Plan, and it must keep at least a 2.50 cumulative chapter GPA to host intake.

Alpha Theta was named a 2025 Standards Achievement Chapter and received the Hawkeye Excellence Award for Excellence in Campus and Community Involvement. About HEGAP · Chapter scorecard

University of Iowa hazing policy

Hazing is prohibited at the University of Iowa. Following Iowa state law, the university defines hazing as intentional or reckless behavior that endangers someone or causes mental or physical discomfort, harassment, or embarrassment, as a condition of joining or staying in an organization. The policy applies whether or not the person consents.

Helping, approving or organizing hazing also violates the policy. Organizations face charges through the Dean of Students, and individuals through the Office of Student Accountability.
